Bill's Best Bets for the Weekend

Weekend of November 21–22, 2026 · Drive times from San Francisco
Best bet this weekend

Lower Sacramento, Redding

Trout are eating midges and Blue Wing Olives. Eggs still produce, but BWO nymphs and rubber legs are the main producers. Steelhead are showing up in greater numbers below Jellys Ferry. Keswick releases are coming down toward 4,000 cfs by Monday, and the weekend storm may cloud the water below the major tributaries, including Clear Creek.

Flies
Best per the report: BP Weiss Nymph, smaller Olive Hot Spot, and small green patterns. Nymphs: Olive Hot Spot #18, Jigged Birds Nest #14–16 (natural or hot spot), Fox's Poopah #12–14, amber Bubbleback Caddis #14, Peaches n' Green, Peaches n' Cream, Ginger Snap, Pat's Rubber Legs #4–8. Eggs: Micro Spawn or Boles Bazookas in Peachy King or Shrimp Pink. Dries: dark Mercer's Missing Link, Spotlight Caddis Emerger, BWO or PMD Comparadun.
Drive
About 3½–4 hours
Flow
About 4,800 cfs below Keswick Dam, scheduled to drop to about 4,000 cfs by Monday; about 9,000 cfs above Bend Bridge with tributary inflow
Weather
Saturday rain, mainly after 10 am (90% chance), high near 45°F; Sunday mostly cloudy with patchy morning fog and a 20% chance of rain after 10 am, high near 50°F
Access
Public wading spots in Redding and Anderson at flows below about 7,500 cfs; most water is fished from a drift boat
Rules
Barbless hooks. Wild trout and wild steelhead must be released.
Full report: The Fly Shop, Nov 17 ›
Steelhead on the move

Trinity River

Rain brought enough water for adult steelhead to move up from below Junction City, and more rain is forecast. Guides are still catching fish on eggs at times, and early summer-run steelhead are taking swung flies.

Flies
Nymphs: Pat's Rubber Legs #4–8 (brown or black), Mercer's Psycho Prince #12–16, TB Solitude Stone #8, Coppertop Duracell #16, Mercer's Gold Bead Biot Epoxy Golden Stone #8–10, red Copper John #10–14, plus Olive Hot Spots, Perdigons and Jigged Holo Points. Eggs: Clown Egg #4–10, Micro Spawn in pink or salmon egg, Boles Bazookas #8. Swing: Ho-Bo Spey #6 (black with green butt), Beaded Assassins, Hoody's Hangers #4, Brett's Klamath Skater #7, Burnt Chickens, Hartwick Hosers, and black-and-blue patterns.
Drive
About 4½–5 hours (Lewiston to Junction City)
Flow
About 300 cfs below Lewiston Dam; about 470 cfs at Douglas City
Weather
Weaverville: Saturday snow likely before 10 am, then rain (100% chance), high near 41°F; Sunday mostly cloudy with areas of fog and a slight chance of rain after 10 am, high near 43°F
Access
Public access sites along Highway 299 and the river road between Lewiston and Junction City
Rules
Hatchery (adipose-clipped) trout and steelhead may be kept; wild fish must be released. Steelhead Report Card required. Rules vary by section; check current regulations.
Full report: The Fly Shop, Nov 17 ›
Trout on the egg bite

Lower Yuba River

Fishing has been strong for several weeks, with tinted water. Salmon are still spawning, so trout are eating fresh and dead eggs, with bugs mixed in from mid-morning. Dry-fly fish have been sipping spent Tricos. If rain from Deer Creek blows the river out, it is reported to clear quickly.

Flies
Subsurface: rubber legs, San Juan Worms, caddis, Prince Nymphs and Perdigon attractors; egg patterns while salmon spawn. Dries: Parachute Adams, crippled caddis and pinkies. The report names no sizes or colors.
Drive
About 2½ hours (Marysville area)
Flow
About 900 cfs near Marysville; the Nov 13 report put the river around 1,000 cfs with a tint
Weather
Saturday mostly cloudy with morning fog and frost, chance of afternoon rain, highs 49–55°F; Sunday mostly cloudy with a slight chance of rain, high around 53°F
Access
Sycamore Ranch, Hammon Grove, and the hike-in on the south side of the Highway 20 bridge
Rules
Closed above the Highway 20 bridge until December 1. Below it: artificial lures, barbless hooks, catch and release. No salmon fishing; stay off spawning beds.
Full report: Fly Fishing Specialties (Capt. Ben Thompson), Nov 13 ›
No long drive

Bay Area Stripers

Stripers are spread through the Bay, with the East Bay (Alameda, Berkeley Bulb, Point Molate) strongest and Crissy Field improving. The water has been very clean, and lighter colors have done better. This is the newest Bay report available.

Flies
Lighter-colored patterns in chartreuse, olive or gray (no specific Bay pattern named). For the Delta, 3/0 Clousers with a rattle.
Drive
0–30 minutes (Crissy Field, Alameda, Point Molate)
Flow
Tides at San Francisco: Saturday high 6.0 ft at 8:15 am, low 0.2 ft at 2:54 pm; Sunday high 6.4 ft at 8:48 am, low −0.5 ft at 3:35 pm
Weather
Saturday rain, highs in the upper 50s; Sunday rain likely, highs in the upper 50s
Access
Public shorelines: Crissy Field in San Francisco; Alameda and Point Molate in the East Bay
Rules
Striped bass size and bag limits apply; check current rules.
Full report: Lost Coast Outfitters, Oct 21 ›
Closest trout water

Putah Creek

Guide Johnny Feaster (Feaster Fishing) reported fall fish getting more active and starting to pair up, with mornings and afternoons most consistent and a slower bite midday. Small nymphs under an indicator produced, including on the larger trout. This is the newest Putah guide report available.

Flies
Beadhead Bird's Nest #18, Umpqua Tungsten Darth Baetis, RS2 #20, Mercer's Micro Mayfly #18 in black or red, green Caddis Pupa #18.
Drive
About 1¼ hours (Winters, Highway 128)
Flow
Gauge readings near Winters were inconsistent this week (about 55–100 cfs); check the current flow before going. The report was written at 300–400 cfs.
Weather
Saturday patchy morning fog, then rain likely, mainly after 4 pm (60% chance), high near 51°F; Sunday mostly cloudy, high near 54°F
Access
Five Yolo County fishing access sites along Highway 128 below Monticello Dam
Rules
Artificial flies and lures, barbless hooks, catch and release all year. Many anglers stay off the creek December 1 to March 1 for spawning.
Full report: Lost Coast Outfitters (Johnny Feaster), Oct 13 ›
